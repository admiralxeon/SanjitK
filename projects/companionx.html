CompanionX

A voice-first AI companion kiosk for care-home residents. It has a live HeyGen avatar, medicine reminders and distress detection, plus a caregiver and family dashboard for vitals, care settings and role-based access.

    L03 · hard decisions

    Six calls that shaped it.

    Most of these trade a little convenience for safety. With older residents and health data, that's the right direction.

    Listening

    The app listens, not the avatar.

    HeyGen's built-in mic is deliberately unused, so the app hears every sentence first. That's how a distress phrase or “send an email to my son” is never missed by a chatbot.

    Trade-off One extra speech-to-text step before the reply.

    Failure modes

    Fail quiet for her, loud for privacy.

    A failed web lookup must never cost her the avatar. Anything that could show one resident's data to another fails hard.

    Health data

    Privacy is scoped per resident.

    • Accounts are split into caretaker and family roles, with access scoped per resident.
    • A break-glass master key stores only its hash. Its sessions last 8 hours, and every use is audited.
    • The backend assigns identity and timestamps on stored events instead of trusting the request.
    • Moving to a new resident closes out the old one and never deletes their records.
    Matching

    Conservative matching.

    The reminder and distress matchers return nothing rather than guess. A false “she's in distress” is worse than asking her to say it again.

    Reminders

    Gentle reminders.

    A medicine reminder asks once, nudges again after 4 minutes, and is logged as missed after 15.

    Within the shared server's limits.
